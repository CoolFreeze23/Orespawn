# OreSpawn for NeoForge 1.21.1 — 2.0.0-beta.5

Version `1.21.1-2.0.0-beta.5`. Part one is for the player, part two for the modder.

## Part one — for the player

The GeckoLib rigs are now the default renderers for every creature except the two robots, and the parity work done since beta.4 ships with them: sizes, hitboxes, textures and targeting are the 1.7.10 original's again.

**Changed**
- Every creature except the Ant Robot and the Spider Robot now renders through a GeckoLib rig converted from its classic model and checked against it pixel for pixel. Nothing should look different. The Queen keeps her hand-made rig.
- One JVM argument puts a species back on its classic renderer for a side-by-side: `-Dorespawn.dev.classicRenderers=ender_knight` (a comma-separated list of registry names, or `classic` for everyone). The start-up log names what was kept classic.

**Fixed (since beta.4)**
- Mob sizes and shadows match 1.7.10 again: the Brutalfly nine times bigger, the Irukandji a quarter of its previous size, the Queen twice what the port drew, and a dozen others corrected.
- Sixty-three mobs have their 1.7.10 hitboxes back. Godzilla is 9.9 wide, Mothra's classic box is 5×2, and the Kraken has its Play Nicely mode.
- Textures on 97 models sit the right way round. The port had mirrored them, which showed on eyes, markings, text and blades.
- The Purple Power orb is a translucent, shimmering ball again, the Coin is visible, and the Crab shows all twenty-four legs and claws.
- OreSpawn's hunters target as they did in 1.7.10: the shared "leave it alone" list, the Peaceful and Play Nicely stand-downs, line of sight through grass and torches, prey lists, hunting ranges and grudges. Four modern targeting switches are on by default, each with one config line to turn it off.

**Good to know**
- The four butterflies share one rig. The Mothra's flat-wing pose differs from the classic drawing by 1.2 % of the image, and that was allowed on purpose.
- A few classic-renderer quirks are copied on purpose and recorded for the next parity pass: the Triffid's facing, Godzilla attacking on every cycle instead of about half, the Prince Teen's opaque wing membranes, the King's wing membranes on his classic renderer, and a Boyfriend or Girlfriend summoned with a negative age.
- The King, the Princess and Godzilla still use a single hitbox. Bone-synced hitbox parts for them are the next phase.
- Fairy Castle Trees in the Crystal dimension can still generate with sheared-off edges at chunk borders. That is the designated first post-beta patch.
- If a creature looks wrong, put it back on its classic renderer with the argument above and post a screenshot through each renderer, the species and the mod version at https://github.com/CoolFreeze23/Orespawn/issues.

**Install:** put `orespawn-1.21.1-2.0.0-beta.5.jar` in `mods/` (NeoForge 21.1, Minecraft 1.21.1, GeckoLib 4.7 or newer). MultiHitboxLib and Databuddy are bundled; the `-slim` jar is the library-less variant. Worlds carry forward from the earlier betas; 1.7.10 worlds are not upgradable.

### In more detail

#### What beta.5 is

beta.5 is the build in which the GeckoLib rigs become the default renderers. Every creature but the two solver robots — the Ant Robot and the Spider Robot — now draws through a GeckoLib rig converted from its classic model and proven against it bone for bone and pixel for pixel; the poses are the classic motion, bit for bit; the Queen keeps her hand-made rig. Underneath the renderer change, the cycle's parity work stands in the same build: the creatures' sizes, shadows, hitboxes and textures, the hunters' targeting and a handful of restored models are the 1.7.10 original's again. And the game is ready for hand-made animations: when clips are delivered for a creature, it knows how to play them.

#### What a default install sees

The rigs. With no argument and no config change, every landed rig draws through its GeckoLib renderer, posed exactly as the classic renderer posed it. The switch that puts a species on its rig is renderer registration, not a change to the creature, and none of the rig batches touched gameplay. Nothing should look different because of the renderer change: a rig that did not match its classic rendering at rest and at a posed sample, pixel for pixel within the comparison's rule, did not ship.

What does look different from beta.4 is the parity work that went into the classic renderers first, in the weeks after beta.4 was cut — and the rigs draw exactly that. In short: mob sizes and shadows are the 1.7.10 registrations again (the Brutalfly nine times bigger; the Irukandji a quarter of its previous size, the Fairy 0.35×, the Robot 3 and the Cricket half, the Hydrolisc 0.65×, a dozen more at 0.75×; the Dragonfly and the Emperor Scorpion 1.5× bigger, the Prince Teen 1.47×; the Queen twice what the port drew, her hit parts on her bones); sixty-three mobs got their 1.7.10 hitboxes back (the Tshirt board 4×4, the Molenoid 3.9×2.6, the Emperor Scorpion 3.5×3, the Sea Monster's box down from 5×5 to 1.25×2.5, the ants, cricket, irukandji and rat small again), Godzilla is 9.9 wide, Mothra's box is 5×2 in classic, and the Kraken has its Play Nicely mode; textures on 97 models sit on their cubes the way the original drew them (the port had mirrored every face's texture on 89 mobs, the four butterflies and seven held items — visible on eyes, markings, text and blades); the Purple Power orb is a translucent, shimmering ball again; the Coin is visible; the Crab shows the twenty-four legs and claws the original drew; and OreSpawn's hunters target as they did in 1.7.10 — the shared "leave it alone" list, the Peaceful and Play Nicely stand-downs, line of sight through grass and torches, prey lists, hunting ranges, who gets picked first, when a grudge is let go — with four modern targeting switches on by default and one config line to turn each off. The full list, grouped, is in part two of the release notes.

#### The switch, and its one-line escape per species

One JVM argument puts a species back on its classic renderer: `-Dorespawn.dev.classicRenderers=<registry names, comma-separated>` — for instance `-Dorespawn.dev.classicRenderers=ender_knight`, or `beaver,elevator` — and the exact token `classic` keeps every species on its classic renderer, for a side-by-side of the whole population. Registry names are trimmed and case-insensitive; the token `classic` is case-exact. The start-up log names each species kept classic, one line per exception at renderer registration; silence means every landed rig is drawing with its GeckoLib renderer. It is a JVM argument, not a config key; the earlier property `orespawn.dev.geckolibRenderers` is no longer read, so a stale one is inert. A species with no landed rig — the Ant Robot, the Spider Robot — never reaches the switch, and the Queen's native rig is untouched either way.

What to report. If a species does look different — a limb on the other side, a part missing, a texture facing the wrong way — put it back on its classic renderer with the argument above and send the side-by-side: the species, the argument, a screenshot through each renderer, and the mod version, at https://github.com/CoolFreeze23/Orespawn/issues. That comparison is exactly the report this beta wants; for a crash, the log as well.

## Part two — for the modder

### The GeckoLib rigs and the counts

**What a converted rig is.** Each classic model is a hand-coded `LayerDefinition` — cubes, pivots, bind rotations, per-face UVs, mirrored parts — carried over from 1.7.10's `ModelBase` classes. The converter executes the compiled `createBodyLayer()`, serialises the baked `ModelPart` tree and writes it as a Bedrock geo with the same bone names, explicit per-face UVs (so a mirrored ModelPart keeps its texel mapping without GeckoLib's native mirror) and, since the mirror fix, in the Bedrock convention the Queen's native rig uses; the rig ships as `assets/orespawn/geo/entity/<name>.geo.json`, byte-identical to the converter's output in the proof tree — the asset audit refuses a drift. The motion is not a clip: a landed rig's pose comes from its hook, the classic `setupAnim` transcribed statement for statement into a `GeoReplacement` descriptor that poses the geo's bones from the same inputs (age, limb swing and amplitude, head yaw and pitch and, for the bipeds, the frame's partial tick), so what the rig does each frame is what the classic code did; fifteen species also ship an exact keyframe transcription of that motion (below). Where the classic renderer drew a part several times under its own transforms (the Rotator's fans, the Purple Power's spoke rings, the Crab's legs, the Giant Robot's twice-drawn limbs), the geo carries one bone per draw; where it applied a whole-model turn before drawing (the Dungeon Beast, the Kraken), the descriptor declares that constant transform; where it drew a second translucent pass (the King's wing membranes), the descriptor declares the pass; and two rigs — the Alien and the Emperor Scorpion — are real parent-child hierarchies whose links were proven one by one.

**How it was proven — the legs, a sentence each,** every one run headlessly on both sides from the pinned jars:

- *Geometry:* every cube corner of the baked geo against the compiled ModelPart tree, in blocks (the maxima on the three trees 1.3e-7 / 1.0e-6 / 2e-6 after the mirror fix).
- *Surface:* every vertex GeckoLib's renderer emits paired with the vertex the vanilla path emits — position, UV and normal (zero-area faces ignored on both sides by decision; the seam lights the true transformed normal, PN-027).
- *Animation:* the hook's bone rotations, positions and hidden flags against the classic `setupAnim` over a grid of ages, amplitudes and entity states (a seeded RNG, the RenderInfo latches, the attacking flags), within the manifests' 2e-6 rad — 0 on every hook but three: the Rotator and the Purple Power at 4.1e-8 rad, the Trooper Bug at 6e-13 rad.
- *Draw order:* the sequence in which the seam draws the bones equals the classic `renderToBuffer` part order, carried in the geo as `orespawn:bone_draw_order` (a hierarchy rig draws parent-first by rule, the deviation from the classic order recorded).
- *Face order:* for a rig that declares `cube_face_order`, the six faces of every cube emitted in the classic order (the Purple Power, the Vortex, the flat-finned rigs).
- *Render state:* one render type per side, compared — cutout for the population, translucent for the Fairy, the Ghost, the Ghost Skelly and the Purple Power, the King's second pass on its own.
- *Reference:* the 1.7.10 `ModelBase` constructor parsed and compared with the compiled port tree — parts, pivots, nesting, initial rotations, texture size — over 109 models: 108 exact, the Chainsaw item model pinned with its two known divergences (ITEM-071).
- *Visual:* both sides rasterised with the game's own cutout rule and compared pixel for pixel at rest and at posed samples — changed fraction ≤ 0.001, mean absolute error ≤ 0.25 — with a pixel whose two front faces are the same pair on both sides counted as pair-contested, never a mismatch, capped at 1 % of the image (the Mothra's declared 1.25 % at one sample the one exception, TEST-019).
- *Keyframe:* for the fifteen transcription species, the shipped clip played through GeckoLib's own loader and processor against the classic formulas, within 2.5e-3 rad, with the wrap sample at every loop seam and the key density an output — the fewest keys that hold the tolerance, one fewer failing (the Catmull-Rom arguments repaired at load in the seam, PN-024).
- *Chain link:* for the two hierarchy rigs, the world transform of every link against the classic model's — 52 links on the Emperor Scorpion, 26 on the Alien, under 1e-6 blocks.

**Where the proofs live.** Three proof chains, each a manifest and a checked-in tree that the build verifies byte for byte on every run: `g1`, `s4` and `t2`; each tree holds the generated geos, `evidence/report.json` with every number above and the visual captures (30 / 198 / 1,755 PNGs); the s4 and t2 trees also hold their models' reference-leg reports, and the standing reference-geometry chain over all 109 models — the g1 pair's reports among them — is `phase_g_reports/reference_proof/` (109 reports). Before the switch-over every rig was also checked by eye on contact sheets: the classic render, the converted rig and their pixel diff at rest and at one posed sample, in the in-game frame.

**The counts.** 104 of the mod's 106 creature rigs are converted and, since the merge, drawn by default — 115 registries in 116 rows on the contact sheets (the Elevator sits on both the g1 pair and Slice 4a), because several registries share a rig: the Ant's five, the Cockateil and the Ruby Bird, the Leon and the Leonopteryx, the Dragon and the Baby Dragon, the Giant Robot and Jeffery, the four butterflies — and the Alien Boss shares the Alien's geo yet counts as its own rig in the 104. The shipped geos are 104 files under `assets/orespawn/geo/entity/`: the 103 seam rigs and the Queen's. The two outside are the Ant Robot and the Spider Robot, whose legs the modern solver places, by design; the Queen is on her own hand-made rig and counted apart. 15 of the 90 artist-tier species carry an exact keyframe transcription of their classic motion — the Beaver, the Tshirt, the Mosquito, the Cliff Racer, the Brutalfly, the Dragonfly, the Cockateil, the Ruby Bird, the Firefly, the Gold Fish, and the Ant rig's five: the Ant, Rainbow Ant, Red Ant, Termite and Unstable Ant — and the rest pose from their hooks.

### The parity fixes, by what a player would have seen

The port's promise is the 1.7.10 original, exactly; its parity work runs from Phase C (2026-06-11) to this cycle. What follows groups it by what a player would have seen, with the issue ids.

**Sizes.** Mobs drew at the wrong size: the port had dropped or retuned the world scale and shadow radius the 1.7.10 renderers registered, so the Brutalfly was a ninth of its size, the Irukandji four times too big, the Fairy nearly three times, the Kraken and the Sea Monster three times, the King and the Queen about half. Every renderer now applies the exact 1.7.10 scale and shadow — 44 world scales and 84 shadows restored across the 133 registrations, the original's baby branches transcribed, every value pinned in the reference gate; the Queen's 2.0 applied after GeckoLib's bone capture so that her hit parts follow her drawn body. Earlier builds had restored the ants' quarter scale (Red Ants and Termites at 0.35×), Mothra's 10× scale, and the Leon and Leonopteryx as one creature at 1.75×.

**Hitboxes.** Hits, spawn fit and collision were off wherever the port's hitbox differed from the 1.7.10 `setSize`: 67 of 145 registrations diverged. Sixty-three plain registrations went back to the original literals in one batch (the Tshirt board 4×4 from 0.6×1.8, the Molenoid 3.9×2.6, the Emperor Scorpion 3.5×3, the Hercules Beetle 3.25×2.75, Robot 2 3×6.2, the Mantis 2.5×3.25, the Trooper Bug 3×3.5, the Urchin 1.35×2.1, the Triffid 2×4, the Coin 1.5×1.5; the Sea Monster down from 5×5 to 1.25×2.5; the ants, cricket, irukandji and rat small again), then Godzilla to 9.9 wide, Mothra to 5×2 in classic (6×3 under the modern key `mothraWideRootHitbox`, MOD-029), the apple cows to the cow line's 0.9×1.3, a small fireball to 0.3125, and the Queen's Play Nicely box to 5.5×6; the Kraken regained its Play Nicely mode (a 1.33×5 box and a third-size draw) and its four Play Nicely behaviour gates. The Queen's ten hurtboxes no longer sit at the world origin for the tick after she spawns, a blow on a wing, a head or a leg does everything a blow on the body does, and she is frustum-culled like any other mob. Earlier: the King and the Queen back to their 22×24 envelope, the Prince Teen to 3.25×4.25 and the Adult to 6.25×10.25, the Alien to 1.1×3.25 (Phase C); the Spider Robot's full 3.25×2.25 body box (2.0.0-beta.1); vanilla creepers' stowaway hitboxes removed (2.0.0-beta.1).

**The mirror.** Two mirrors, one in the classic models and one in the converter. On 97 models — 89 mobs, the butterfly family and seven held items — the port had turned the 1.7.10 sources' `mirror = true` into a mirror flag that actually took effect, so every face sampled its texture flipped left for right relative to the original (in 1.7.10 the flag was set after the box was built and never reached it, proven from the 1.7.10 client jar itself); invisible on symmetric texels, visible on eyes, markings, text and blades. Stage 1 dropped it on the Ender Reaper for an in-game A/B (5354420); stage 2 dropped the 3,234 calls that remained, geometry-only, with the reference leg proving 0 mirror divergences over 109 models (b069020). Then the converter's own mirror: every rig drawn through the seam had been the left-right mirror of its classic rendering, 0.009 blocks higher, because the converter negated x where GeckoLib's baker negates it again, and the comparison looked at both sides in a frame where the mirror could not show. The converter now writes rigs in the Bedrock convention the Queen's hand-made rig uses; 77 geos, 15 keyframe clips, 109 reference geos and 298 reference clips were regenerated; the probe reproduces both real render chains from the jars, so its frame is the in-game frame and a mirrored rig fails; the seam compensates the residual height so the two chains agree to float precision; and in-game looks at the Ender Knight's one sword — the same hand, the same height, through both renderers — accepted it (e242357; closed with the merge, 8d7fd2d).

**Targeting.** Over four waves and their follow-ups every OreSpawn hunter went back to the 1.7.10 rules: the shared "leave it alone" list is the original twelve again and all 38 of its call sites are back (twenty-six hunters had lost the screen and would chase a butterfly or an ant); hunters that had been scanning players only scan every living thing in the original box again, ranked by the original sorter (a creeper's distance halved, a big creature outranking a smaller one closer in); creative players are told apart the 1.7.10 way, by creative mode and never by "cannot be hurt"; the Peaceful and Play Nicely stand-downs are back on the five hunters vanilla never despawns and on forty-three more, read live; line of sight is the 1.7.10 ray, stopped by grass, flowers and torches, with the feet-level second ray before a bite; prey lists, hunting ranges and boxes, who gets picked first, when a grudge is forgotten and when a scan's pick is dropped are the original's; the Ender Knight and Reaper have their enderman reflexes, their stare rules, their portal sounds and the Knight's wet teleport back; the Kraken's grab, exclusions, health-keyed behaviour and tie-breaks match; the Luna Moth flies its own loop; the Stinky tunnels toward coal ore again and burps — deepslate coal ore too, the modern engine's two blocks for 1.7.10's one (PN-021). Six modern switches, on by default, are labelled as additions and not parity — `petsDefendOwner`, `godzillaSparesBossPeers`, `pointysaurusStareAggro`, `cryolophosaurusRevengeChase`, `fireRespectsMobGriefing` and `chainsawSweepVanillaSight` (MOD-031 to MOD-035, MOD-037) — each `false`, or `modern.enabled = false`, the 1.7.10 behaviour, and the Valentine's Day Girlfriend spares Peaceful and creative players in both modes (MOD-036). Two engine-frame differences are recorded rather than coded (PN-020, PN-022). Phase C had already restored the prey lists and target filters of the Cephadrome, the Leaf Monster and the Prince.

**The models restored.** Where the port's model was not the original's, the original came back: the Coin (the port's model was invisible; the 1.7.10 quad at 0.125 scale, BUG-040); the Vortex's eight residual divergences (ENT-S-089); ten models regenerated or re-expressed from the parsed 1.7.10 constructors — the Island, IslandToo, Skate, Mosquito, Ghost, Stink Bug, Elevator, CaterKiller, Tshirt and Sea Viper (ENT-S-091); the per-entity RenderInfo restored on fourteen species — the Alien, Cave Fisher, Cephadrome, Dragon, Dungeon Beast, Emperor Scorpion, Ghost Skelly, Leon, Lurking Terror, Nastysaurus, Ostrich, Pitch Black, Scorpion and Prince Teen — after the Kraken's pattern, so two Cave Fishers snap their claws on their own cycles instead of sharing one latch, and the formula divergences of the Cave Fisher, Cephadrome, Dragon, Dungeon Beast, Leon, Ostrich and Prince Teen transcribed line for line (ENT-S-093); the Elevator drawn as the 1.7.10 plain Render — no hurt tint, no name tag, no living rotations (ENT-S-094); the Purple Power orb translucent, glowing and shimmering, its three rings of spokes re-rolled every frame (ENT-S-146); the Crab's twenty-four legs and claws (ANIM-025); the pressure-plate override on fifteen entities (ENT-S-090); the Prince Teen's 0.32 pace (ENT-S-156); the Dragonfly's hurt re-aim and steering and the Stinky's and Spyro's flight-target reach (ENT-S-148, ENT-S-149, ENT-S-151). Earlier phases had fixed the 39-model `wingspeed` mistranslation (ANIM-001), the Rotator's 24-blade gyroscope, the Giant Robot's walk and windmill punch, the Spider Robot's eight legs and the Ant Robot's six, the Rat's tail (Phase C), and the rat's and the adult Prince's textures and the Instant Garden crops (the 1.0 line). Four classic poses that lost motion in the port — the Gamma Metroid, the Rubber Ducky, the Terrible Terror and the Cricket — and the Chainsaw's blade tip are recorded for the parity pass to come, and the sheets of those four say what the original moved more (ANIM-021 to ANIM-024, ITEM-071).

**The terrain and the world.** The earlier builds' parity fixes stand under this one: the Chaos dimension's floating islands over open void, its hills and mountains, grass on every island, safe arrivals and working beds (the inverted density field, the cell-versus-block noise scale and the /128 normalisation, all verified against the decompiled engine, 2.0.0-beta.4); the Village and Mining dimension lake crash (2.0.0-beta.3); the Mining dimension's daylight, the ants' natural spawns and despawning, the anthills' look and the termites' replication (2.0.0-beta.2); the wild royal spawns and the Queen's mid-air freeze (2.0.0-beta.1).

### The harness and the proofs

The build checks itself, and every change was verified on the tree it committed: the drift check (the smoke benchmark's provenance pin over `build.gradle`, `tools/g1_model_proofs.json`, `tools/g1_performance_benchmark.json`, `tools/g1_benchmark_gate.py`, `G1PerformanceBenchmark.java` and `G1AnimationRuntime.java`, plus the runtime classpath and representative class hashes), `gradlew build` — which runs the asset audit, the three parity chains in verify mode against their checked-in trees, the reference-geometry and renderer-pin legs, the reference-clip verify (311 files), the weights blend probe and the Queen part-placement probe, then builds the jar — and `runGameTestServer`, whose literal `All N required tests passed` line is the suite's verdict (Gradle alone exits 0 even when the server stops before a test runs, which is why the literal is the check). At this build: `All 1293 required tests passed`, in both robot modes (the count only grows — 192 at the start of Phase G, 1293 with the Butterfly rig's `t2_015`); the asset audit green — `RESULT: 0 error(s)`, nothing at its warning level, 4 acknowledged, `draw order: 104 shipped geo: 103 seam + 1 outside-seam` (the audit refuses a shipped rig that drifts from its proof, a rig without its draw-order key, a flat-cube rig without its face order, and a reference clip inside the jar); the parity verifies `G1 PARITY PASS` at 2 / 13 / 101 models with every checked-in proof verified byte for byte; and the jar `build/libs/orespawn-1.21.1-2.0.0-beta.5.jar` beside the slim jar. The benchmark: the component smoke benchmark pinned under `phase_g_reports/g1_proof/benchmark/` is a provenance check, labelled `SMOKE_ONLY / COMPONENT_PROXY_ONLY / PENDING_LIVE_PRECUTOVER`, and makes no performance claim; the spawn-100 live harness (`/orespawn bench` under `-Dorespawn.dev.bench=true`: six isolation scenes, paired classic / GeckoLib reports of frame time, allocation, GC, server MSPT, the MultiHitboxLib counters and packet bytes) and its adopted thresholds (the collector's bytes per walk must not rise; the mixed scene's median frame time within +10 % and its p95 within +2 ms; server p95 MSPT under 50 ms; no sustained packet growth) are for live testing; there is no live report at this build. Two performance fixes did land with proofs of their own: the per-entity animation state that used to be held for every mob ever drawn is evicted when a mob leaves the loaded world and cleared on leaving a world (OPT-029, measured through the counters), and the MultiHitboxLib harvest — the per-entity collection stamp, the render hook that fired twice per bone, the player-attack part unwrapping, conservative cull bounds (BUG-044, OPT-028, OPT-030).
